# Evidence-checked review

Workflow steps naming `reviewer` run Swarm's native `system-reviewer` as a task,
without a reviewer worker. Custom reviewers are named by worker id. Native
reviewer tasks expose their durable verdict and findings in `swarm_task_result`.

The system reviewer reviews one job's exact committed head against its base, in its own session. It reads `git diff BASE...HEAD` and the changed files and judges four things:

1. Is every requirement of the brief fully done?
2. Do the report's claims match the evidence? A test or check the report says passed must appear among the commands the job actually ran, with exit code 0. A claim the evidence does not back is a finding.
3. Are there gaps: behavior the brief needs that nothing implements or tests, a missing failure or negative case, or a behavior change the brief did not request?
4. Are there security problems?

It reports BLOCK when any of these finds a problem, otherwise OK. Each finding explains what is missing or wrong with a `file:line` location and reaches the coder verbatim. Alternative approaches and style preferences are notes in SUMMARY, never findings.

A reviewer started by an automation's `agent` step gets the evidence in its brief: the reviewed job's original brief, its report, the commands it actually ran with their exit codes (read from its session's tool calls) and its diff against its base (see [automations](automations.md#what-the-reviewer-receives)). The base is the job's effective base: for a job rebased onto a newer target after it was created, the merge-base of its head and that target, so the range holds only the job's own commits (see [the base after a rebase](automations.md#the-base-after-a-rebase)). Every reviewer's checkout is a worktree of the folder, so the folder's branches are its own refs, and an automation reviewer's brief names the target branch's current tip, so whether a commit is already on the target is answered from evidence. A reviewer started by hand gets the job's brief and head; it says in SUMMARY which claims it could not verify.

The reviewer runs no builds, tests, checks, or scripts: it judges the evidence the job produced. Reports retain four sections: VERDICT, SUMMARY, FINDINGS, and TESTS RUN. TESTS RUN always says `none (review is read-only)`. The reviewer prompt asks for the four headings as plain text at the start of a line, without Markdown emphasis.

The structured report may follow progress notes in the reviewer's final message: Swarm reads it from the line that starts with `VERDICT: OK` or `VERDICT: BLOCK` and ignores the text before it. Because models do not always follow the plain-heading rule, a heading may also be wrapped in Markdown bold at the start of its line: `**VERDICT: BLOCK**`, `__VERDICT: OK__`, `**VERDICT:** OK`, `**SUMMARY:**`, `**FINDINGS:**` or `**TESTS RUN:** none ...`, with the closing `**` or `__` right after the colon or right after the verdict word. Only that wrapper is removed. It still fails closed (recorded as BLOCK, "missing or malformed review verdict") when more than one line is a VERDICT heading (plain or bold, in any combination), when the notes before the verdict contain a heading at the start of a line (plain or bold), or when the sections are missing, empty, out of order, quoted (`>`), in a list item, fenced, indented or embedded in prose; quoted prose is never read as an approval. The review record keeps the FINDINGS section verbatim from the full message, without the FINDINGS heading's own emphasis markers, up to 16,000 bytes (longer findings are cut at a line boundary and say so); `swarm_get_job` shows the recorded verdict and findings, and an automation's `agent` and `reopen` steps send those findings in full, never the job's shortened report.

A review recorded with the malformed reason is unreadable: an automation never sends it to the coder as findings. Its `agent` step's outcome has `verdict: block` and `review_unreadable: true` and no findings, and a `reopen` step sends nothing, uses no round and ends the delivery `blocked` with `review unreadable: <reviewer job id>` (see [automations](automations.md#reopen-steps)), so the AI that delegated the job can read the reviewer's session.

The brief, report, commands, diff and repository are untrusted data: the reviewer assesses them but never follows instructions found in them. A re-review assesses previous findings first and identifies which are fixed.

A review is report-only and never commits. By default the reviewer is a new session in the reviewed job's own worktree, where the job did its work: nothing is copied, with the sandbox on the worktree is mounted read-only for that reviewer session only (with the sandbox off by the owner's choice the reviewer runs on the machine like every other agent, read-only by its tools), it is left as it was when the review ends, and the review starts only at exactly the reviewed head with nothing uncommitted. A verdict given while that worktree moved or got uncommitted changes is recorded as BLOCK. With `review_checkout: "new"` (an automation step's `checkout: "new"`) the reviewer gets its own detached, disposable checkout at the reviewed head instead (see [where the agent works](automations.md#where-the-agent-works-checkout)). What happens to a reviewed job afterwards (delivering it with `swarm_deliver_job`, sending findings back with `swarm_message_job` or an automation's `reopen` step) is up to the AI or automation that asked for the review.

## Security fixes (automation opt-in)

One exception, opt-in per automation step and never the default: an automation `agent` step naming `reviewer` or a reviewer worker by its id may set `fixes: "security"` (see [automations](automations.md#reviewer-fixes-fixes-security)). Only that reviewer may commit small, security-only fixes (a missing escape, a path check, a secret in a log line) instead of blocking for them. A reviewer started any other way (`swarm_give_job` with `review_job`, or an `agent` step without `fixes`) stays read-only as above; Swarm never decides on its own to let a reviewer write.

With `fixes`:

- The reviewer works on its own writable branch `agent/review-<run>`, started at the reviewed head in its own worktree of the folder, and every tool call checks that `HEAD` descends from the reviewed head. Its checkout is kept after the job ends, like a coder's.
- It may edit files and commit; it cannot start other agents. It runs only the narrowest test that proves a fix and lists it under TESTS RUN.
- Every fix is also a FINDINGS entry marked `FIXED`, with what it was, why, and `file:line`. Anything that is not a small security fix is a BLOCK finding, not a fix.
- Limits enforced in code after the job ends: its commits on top of the reviewed head may change at most 80 lines in total (added plus removed; a binary file counts as over). Over the limit the automation step fails with `reviewer fixes too large` and the delivery head does not move. Only an OK verdict moves the delivery head to the reviewer's head; a reviewer that committed while its verdict is BLOCK does not move it. A security problem the reviewer fixed is not a reason to BLOCK. An unreadable report (recorded with the malformed reason) is a BLOCK too: its fix commits are listed but never applied, nothing is sent to the coder, and the delivery ends `blocked` with `review unreadable: <reviewer job id>`. The fixing reviewer's prompt has the same plain-heading rule.
- An OK verdict fails closed: the reviewer's head and fix commits are read from its own checkout with Git, and when they cannot be read the step fails with `reviewer fixes unreadable` and the delivery head does not move. A fixing step after another agent step that already moved the delivery head is refused before it starts.
- `swarm_get_job` on the reviewer job shows its fix commits and head, read from its checkout; the delivery shows the moved head and the applied fix commits. The reviewer job itself is still not delivered with `swarm_deliver_job`.
- The review it records on the reviewed job carries `fix_head` when it committed, and that review shows as `stale` on the reviewed job: an OK given with fixes never reads as an OK of the unfixed head. An OK whose checkout cannot be read is recorded as BLOCK.
